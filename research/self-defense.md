## Question

Would learning practical self-defense, potentially through Krav Maga, increase my ability to protect myself and Lydia and handle difficult situations with more confidence and better judgment?

## Why it might matter

This is about acquiring a durable capability, not seeking confrontation. If a genuinely difficult situation occurs, having trained responses could reduce dependence on improvisation and increase our ability to disengage, protect each other, and get to safety.

## Evidence

- [user assertion] I am concerned about situations that could arise around Ceuta and want greater preparedness for difficult situations.
- [user assertion] I want to be able to defend myself and Lydia if necessary.
- [user assertion] We are currently looking at Krav Maga options.
- [user assertion] Learning self-defense is a capability I consider worth developing.
- [inference] Regular training could improve confidence, physical readiness, and decision-making under stress, but the magnitude of that benefit depends on the quality and realism of the training.

## Uncertainty

It is not yet clear which training option would provide the best practical preparation, how much training is required before the capability becomes reliable, or whether Krav Maga is the best fit compared with other approaches.

## Next experiment

Compare the available Krav Maga options on training quality, instructor credibility, realistic pressure training, safety, consistency, logistics, and fit with the capability we actually want to acquire. Prefer evidence from trial sessions or direct observation over marketing claims.
