## Question

Would learning to drive allow me to assume driving when Lydia cannot, increasing our mobility and making it easier to have better weekends together?

## Why it might matter

Driving is a practical capability that can reduce our dependence on Lydia being able to drive at a given moment. If I can reliably take over, anxiety or inability to drive on a particular day does not have to constrain where we can go or whether we go at all.

## Evidence

- [user assertion] Learning to drive is a necessary skill for me.
- [user assertion] Lydia has anxiety and sometimes cannot drive.
- [inference] My ability to drive could increase our shared autonomy by allowing me to take over when needed.
- [inference] Greater driving autonomy could create more options for spending better weekends together.

## Uncertainty

The practical value depends on how often Lydia cannot drive, how much this currently constrains our plans, and whether learning to drive would materially change the range or frequency of activities we can choose together.

## Next experiment

Observe real weekend situations where driving capacity constrains or changes a plan, and use them as evidence for whether acquiring this capability deserves more attention.
