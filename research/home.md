## Question

Can Lydia and I move toward a suitable home — ideally an independent chalet with four bedrooms and a small plot, close enough to everyday services — without taking on financial strain that compromises our autonomy?

## Why it might matter

A shared home is a major long-term decision affecting autonomy, quality of life, mobility, and financial capacity. The objective is not simply to find a property we like, but to find a sustainable point where the home supports the life we want without becoming a maintenance or financial burden.

## Evidence

- [user assertion] I am currently looking for a chalet for Lydia and me to live in together.
- [user assertion] I have approximately €38,000 in savings.
- [user assertion] €38,000 is not sufficient on its own to buy the kind of four-bedroom house with a small plot we want.
- [user assertion] The search has considered budgets up to approximately €400,000.
- [user assertion] Preferred property characteristics include an independent house, four or more bedrooms, two or more bathrooms, more than 100 m², and a small-to-moderate plot.
- [user assertion] We want practical access to the centre and everyday services, rather than a property that requires driving for basic errands.
- [user assertion] We want to avoid oversized, high-maintenance properties.
- [user assertion] Lydia's uncle is expected to move out in approximately one month and can no longer keep Tina.
- [user assertion] Tina currently approaches windows and could fall if they are left unsecured.
- [inference] The relevant problem is therefore a financing-and-property-fit question, not merely a property search.
- [inference] If Tina is to move into the shared home, window protection is a concrete prerequisite for doing so safely.

## Uncertainty

The main uncertainty is what purchase price and financing structure can provide the desired home while preserving enough financial resilience after purchase. It is also unclear which compromises — location, house size, plot, condition, or timing — provide the best balance between the desired life and sustainable cost.

There is also a near-term practical dependency: the home must be safe for Tina before she moves in, particularly with respect to accessible windows.

## Next experiment

Establish the realistic purchase envelope from available savings, sustainable financing, transaction costs, and required post-purchase reserve. Then compare that envelope with the actual supply of suitable properties in the preferred areas, prioritizing low-maintenance homes with practical access to services.

In parallel, establish what window protection is required and install the necessary mosquito screens or other secure protection before Tina moves in.
